Why did ancient Chinese people believe that extreme fear could change a person forever?
The answer comes from an ancient idea about the human soul.
They believed that a person was not made of only a body.
A human being also had invisible spiritual parts called:
Hun and Po.
Together, they controlled things like:
consciousness,
emotions,
memory,
and the ability to think.
Normally, these parts stayed together and kept a person whole.
But according to Chinese folklore,
a sudden and overwhelming shock could disturb this balance.
Some parts of the soul could be frightened away.
And if the part connected to the mind was affected,
a person might become confused,
lose memories,
or seem completely different.
This was called:
“losing one's soul from fear.”
To restore the person,
ancient traditions developed rituals called soul calling.
By calling the person's name and performing ceremonies,
people believed the lost soul could return.
So in traditional Chinese belief,
fear was not just an emotion.
It was something powerful enough to affect the soul itself.
