Why did ancient Chinese people spend so much time choosing the perfect place to bury the dead?
Because they believed a grave was not just a place for the dead.
It could affect the living.
In traditional Chinese belief,
death did not completely separate humans from the natural world.
After a person died,
their body returned to the earth,
but the connection between the person, the land, and the universe continued.
According to ancient Feng Shui beliefs,
the environment surrounding a grave could influence the balance between ancestors and their descendants.
A place with harmonious energy,
balanced landscapes,
and the right connection with nature
could bring blessings to future generations.
But a place with disturbed energy
could bring misfortune to the family.
This was why choosing a burial location was considered extremely important.
And this belief also created one of China's most famous legends:
the Jiangshi.
Ancient people believed that under certain unusual conditions,
a corpse could undergo a mysterious transformation
and become a creature between life and death.
So choosing a burial place was not only about honoring the dead.
It was also about protecting the living.
Because in the traditional Chinese worldview,
death does not end the relationship between humans and nature.
