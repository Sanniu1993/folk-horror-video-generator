Many people think Feng Shui is just superstition.
But if we go back thousands of years,
we discover something interesting.
Ancient Chinese people did not have modern science,
they had no air quality sensors,
no environmental data,
and no advanced technology.
But they spent generations observing one question:
What kind of places are better for humans to live?
For example,
traditional Feng Shui says:
a house that has suffered a serious fire should not be lived in again.
Ancient people could not explain why.
But they noticed a pattern:
people living in these places often experienced problems.
Today, modern science tells us that fires can damage building materials,
and leave behind harmful substances that may affect indoor environments.
Similar observations existed in choosing where to build a house.
Ancient Chinese studied:
sunlight,
wind direction,
water sources,
terrain,
and the surrounding environment.
Over generations,
these experiences were collected and developed into a system called Feng Shui.
It is not modern science with mathematical formulas.
But it represents something very human:
the attempt to understand how our environment affects our lives.
Before modern science existed,
humans used the tools they had to understand the world.
Some ancient observations were later explained by science.
Others became cultural beliefs.
And Feng Shui represents one of humanity's oldest questions:
How are humans connected to the world around them?
